The signal moves. The stream holds.

Keel is a cloud stack that sits between your phone and Twitch, Kick or YouTube. When your signal drops, viewers see a clean Be right back screen. When it returns, you are live again. The broadcast never stops.

Your viewers do not see your signal. They see your stream.

Most mobile streaming fails in public: the platform sees the ingest die, the VOD splits, viewers leave. Keel puts a stable broadcaster in the cloud, so the platform only ever talks to a connection that does not drop.

Three states

One mark. Three tilts.

Live.

Signal is good.

Your phone feed goes straight through. Tilt: 6 degrees. Nothing to do.

Weak signal.

Below 800 kbps for 3 seconds.

A small pill tells viewers you are on a weak connection. The feed keeps playing. Tilt: 12 degrees.

Holding on.

Below 50 kbps for 2 seconds.

Be right back screen goes up. The broadcast to the platform continues. Back to live only after 5 stable seconds. Tilt: 18 degrees.

The operator view

A dashboard you can read at arm's length.

Why rebuild this

Streamers should not have to debug their tools live.

Existing hosted toolkits work until the moment they do not. Glitches and dead sessions happen in front of tens of thousands of viewers.

Keel is built around one idea: degrade fast, recover slow. Switch to Be right back within seconds of a real drop, and wait for a stable signal before going back. No flapping, no half-frozen frames.

Questions

Straight answers.

Does it work today?

The core path is tested end to end in a container: SRT in, OBS switching between LIVE, LOW SIGNAL and BRB, broadcast out, recovery, zero unexpected stops. It has not yet been tested on a real phone over cellular or against live Twitch, Kick or YouTube. The status page lists exactly what is and is not proven.

Which apps can send to it?

Anything that speaks SRT or RTMP, which includes the common IRL streaming apps. Bonded cellular (SRTLA) is supported through a receiver in the stack, still to be field tested.

What does it cost to run?

A CPU server with x264 is the cheapest start. A GPU server is for heavier scenes. The investor page has a cost model with every assumption visible and editable.

Can I self host?

Yes. One docker compose file brings up the whole stack on any Linux server with a public IP. The guide takes about thirty minutes.

Who is behind it?

It is an open project, designed so a funded team can take it to launch in about a week. See the launch plan.
